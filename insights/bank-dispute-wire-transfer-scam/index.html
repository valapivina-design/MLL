Bank Dispute After a Wire Transfer Scam or Fake Broker

Wires move fast and are hard to reverse. If you sent a bank transfer to a fake broker or investment pitch, a same-day or next-day recall attempt still matters — even when success rates are low.

Act with the sending bank immediately

Ask for a wire recall / recall request and a fraud escalation. Provide beneficiary details, reference numbers, and why you believe the payee is fraudulent. Delays of even a few business days sharply reduce odds.

What banks usually cannot do

Once funds are withdrawn by the beneficiary, banks often cannot pull money back unilaterally. That does not end the file — police reports, civil claims, and tracing can still matter — but the recall clock is separate and short.

Frequently asked questions

Straight answers on next steps, deadlines, and when a confidential consultation may help.

Can a bank reverse a wire to a scam broker?

Sometimes if you act immediately and funds remain at the receiving bank. Many recalls fail once money is moved onward. Still request the recall and keep the reference.

What evidence should I give the bank?

Beneficiary name and account, transfer references, the domain or app you used, chats promising returns or blocking withdrawals, and any police report number.

Is a failed recall the end?

No. Reporting, tracing, and civil options may remain. A confidential consultation can help sequence those steps after the bank process.
