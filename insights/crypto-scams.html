Crypto scams and bitcoin scam risks

When someone asks whether bitcoin is a scam, they usually mean a specific pitch — a fake exchange, mining contract, or unlicensed trading desk — not the bitcoin protocol itself. This brief separates ordinary asset risk from the fraud patterns we see in intake.

What “crypto scam” usually means

A crypto scam is not “bitcoin went down.” It is a scheme that induces deposits through deception: cloned trading platforms, fabricated returns, fake customer support, or wallets controlled by the promoter. Cryptocurrency scams reuse the same playbook across WhatsApp, Telegram, dating apps, and cold-call “brokers.”

Common variants include fake spot exchanges, “AI trading” dashboards, cloud-mining contracts, and advance-fee traps after the first loss. Public complaints often cluster around withdrawal freezes and sudden “tax” or “AML release” fees.

Cited pattern — FTX (2022): The FTX Trading Ltd collapse and related bankruptcy proceedings remain a high-profile reference for exchange failure, customer-fund disputes, and why “brand recognition” alone is not due diligence. See our FTX brief in the Company & Broker Index.

Is bitcoin a scam?

Is bitcoin a scam? Bitcoin as a public network is not a scam. Many bitcoin scam operations borrow the word “bitcoin” to sell unlicensed trading accounts, mining packages, or “guaranteed” doubles. Treat any unsolicited offer that requires sending crypto to a stranger’s address as high risk.

Is bitcoin mining legit?

Is bitcoin mining legit when you run hardware or buy hashpower from a known operator with verifiable payouts? Sometimes. Cloud-mining sites that promise fixed daily percentages with no verifiable hashrate, no company trail, and locked withdrawals are classic investment-scam wrappers. Ask for the domain, company registration, and payout history before funding.

How victims usually discover the fraud

  • Profits appear on-screen but withdrawals fail or require new deposits.
  • Support moves the conversation off the app onto Telegram or WhatsApp.
  • The story changes when you ask for a withdrawal — new fees, new “compliance,” same blocked funds.

Frequently asked questions

Straight answers on reporting a scam, recovery options, and when a confidential consultation may help.

Is bitcoin a scam?

No — bitcoin is a public network. Most “bitcoin scam” cases involve fake brokers, mining contracts, or unlicensed desks using the bitcoin name. Focus on the entity that took your funds and the wallet addresses used.

Are all crypto scams the same as exchange hacks?

No. Many cryptocurrency scams are social-engineering frauds: you send funds willingly to an address controlled by the promoter. Hacks and account takeovers are different fact patterns and need different evidence.

Is bitcoin mining legit?

Industrial or self-hosted mining can be legitimate. Fixed-return “cloud mining” sold through ads with no verifiable operator is a frequent crypto scam pattern. Verify the company, domain, and payout rails before depositing.

What should I save after a crypto scam?

Transaction IDs, wallet addresses, chat exports, website URLs, deposit receipts, and any KYC documents you were asked to send. Those records support reports and any later consultation.
