Digital forensics and blockchain analysis

Digital forensics preserves the story of what happened on devices and accounts. Blockchain analysis maps where value moved after a deposit — two tools that support reporting and civil strategy.

Why digital forensics matters in scam files

Digital forensics in an investment or crypto matter is usually about integrity: chat exports with metadata, original URLs, email headers, screenshots that include full addresses, and device logs that show which app was installed. Without that, a “report scam” filing is thin and a consultation starts from scratch.

Blockchain analysis in plain language

Blockchain analysis follows transaction IDs across addresses. Analysts look for consolidation wallets, peel chains, bridges, and deposits into known exchange clusters. The goal is not magic recovery — it is attribution and timing for freeze letters or law-enforcement packets when the facts support them.

Cited use-case: In pig-butchering and fake-broker matters, victims often have dozens of small deposits. Linking those txs to a common cluster is how investigators decide whether a platform complaint, criminal report, or civil defendant search is realistic.

What we ask clients to collect

  1. Full wallet addresses and every tx hash.
  2. Exact domains and app names used (character-by-character).
  3. Chat exports from WhatsApp, Telegram, SMS, or email.
  4. Bank or card statements for fiat on-ramps.

Frequently asked questions

Straight answers on reporting a scam, recovery options, and when a confidential consultation may help.

What is digital forensics in a crypto scam case?

It is the careful preservation and examination of devices, chats, emails, and URLs so the timeline of a crypto scam can be shown to banks, police, or counsel without relying on memory alone.

Does blockchain analysis get crypto back by itself?

No. Blockchain analysis shows movement. Getting value back still needs a freeze, a cooperative intermediary, a defendant with assets, or another legal pathway.

When should forensics start?

As soon as you suspect fraud. Overwriting phones, deleting chat apps, or “resetting” wallets destroys evidence that later reports depend on.
