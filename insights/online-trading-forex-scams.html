Online trading scams and forex fraud patterns

Online trading is not inherently a scam — but unlicensed clones, manipulated apps, and pump-and-dump rooms regularly target retail traders.

Is online trading a scam?

Is online trading a scam? Regulated brokers exist and publish licence numbers you can verify on official registers. A forex trading scam typically uses a lookalike site, offshore shell, or app that shows profits until you try to withdraw. The product name (“forex,” “CFDs,” “stocks”) is cover.

Cited pattern — brand clones: Our index includes clone-tier briefs such as lookalike “LATAM” or typo domains wearing real broker names. Always compare the domain character-by-character with the official site before depositing. Browse the index if a name already carries public warnings.

Pump and dump scheme forex

A pump and dump scheme forex or signal-group pitch coordinates buyers into thin markets, then sells into the rush. Victims see early screenshots of gains; exits fail or slippage erases the account. Related stock-room pumps use the same social mechanics.

Checklist before you fund

  • Verify the licence on the regulator’s site — not a PDF on the broker’s domain.
  • Search the exact domain for warnings.
  • Reject any request for more deposits to “unlock” a withdrawal.

Frequently asked questions

Straight answers on reporting a scam, recovery options, and when a confidential consultation may help.

Is online trading a scam?

Not inherently. Many scams impersonate trading. Check licences on official registers and treat withdrawal-fee demands as a major red flag.

What is a forex trading scam?

A forex trading scam is typically an unlicensed or cloned platform that induces deposits with fake performance and blocks withdrawals, sometimes while using a real brand’s name.

What is a pump and dump scheme in forex?

Coordinated buying that inflates a thin price, followed by insiders selling. Retail traders are left holding the loss when liquidity disappears.
