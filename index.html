Litigation Questions, Answered

Common questions from clients considering suit, facing a claim, or evaluating settlement.

When should I speak with a litigation attorney?

As soon as a dispute looks likely to escalate—before key documents disappear, statutes of limitation run, or a demand letter forces a rushed response. Early advice often preserves options and reduces cost.

Do most cases go to trial?

No. The majority of civil disputes resolve through negotiation, mediation, or motion practice. Still, the strongest settlements usually come from cases prepared as if they will be tried.

How are litigation fees structured?

Depending on the matter, we may work on hourly, flat-fee phases, or contingency arrangements where permitted. We discuss fee options during the free case evaluation so expectations are clear from the start.

What should I bring to a consultation?

Contracts, emails, demand letters, pleadings already filed, insurance policies, timelines of events, and a list of key witnesses. Incomplete files slow strategy; organized materials accelerate it.

Can you help if a lawsuit has already been filed?

Yes. We regularly step in after service of a complaint, during discovery, or when a prior counsel relationship ends. Deadlines still control, so contact us promptly once you are served.
